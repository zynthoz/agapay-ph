# Vulnerability through Informal Employment (Decent Work Deficit) in the Philippines

## Overview
A large portion of workers in the Philippines operate in the informal sector, facing no job security, unstable income, and no benefits like health insurance or pensions.[web:1][web:2][web:4] This creates a decent work deficit, marked by poor working conditions, low productivity, and exclusion from legal protections.[web:4][web:6] Informal employment directly heightens vulnerability, limiting social protection and upward mobility while exposing workers to crises such as COVID-19 job losses.[web:1][web:8]

## Key Characteristics
- **Prevalence**: Informal workers dominate the economy, especially in rural areas and BARMM, with high rates of low-paid, temporary jobs that evade minimum wage and employment laws.[web:1][web:2]
- **Income Instability**: Reliance on seasonal or market-driven work leads to uncertain earnings and financial exclusion from credit or services.[web:1][web:4]
- **Lack of Benefits**: No access to health insurance, retirement, unemployment support, or safety nets, amplifying risks during illness, disability, or economic shocks.[web:1][web:2][web:5]

## Connections to Vulnerability

### Lack of Social Protection
Informal workers miss essential safeguards, leaving them without insurance or safety nets during downturns.[web:1][web:6] Studies highlight this deficit as critical, with many unable to hedge against shocks like the COVID-19 pandemic.[web:4][web:8] Efforts like the Domestic Workers Act aim to include some, but coverage remains limited.[web:5]

### Limited Upward Mobility
Low skills, poor training access, and weak bargaining power trap workers in low-productivity roles.[web:2][web:4] About 90% of low-paid jobs are informal, blocking transitions to formal employment with better prospects.[web:2] This perpetuates poverty, especially for rural and unskilled laborers.[web:2]

### Crisis Vulnerability
During crises like COVID-19, informal workers suffered disproportionate job and income losses due to unsafe conditions and no protections.[web:4][web:8] Economic recessions expose them to higher poverty risks without collective representation or financial services.[web:8]

## Globalization's Impact
Globalization drives contractualization, labor flexibilization, and informalization through trade openness.[web:3][web:7] Export growth shifts demand toward low-skilled production jobs, benefiting women somewhat but increasing low-wage informality.[web:7] It also fuels OFW dependence, as domestic opportunities shrink in inefficient sectors, heightening vulnerability to global market fluctuations.[web:3][web:7]

| Aspect | Informal Sector Impact | Globalization Link |
|--------|------------------------|--------------------|
| Job Security | Temporary/casual roles, no protections [web:2] | Rise in export-oriented, flexible labor [web:7] |
| Income | Low/unstable, 56% low-paid [web:2] | Trade shifts to low-skill jobs [web:3] |
| Social Protection | No insurance/benefits [web:1] | Informalization erodes formal safeguards [web:6] |
| Crises | High job losses (e.g., COVID) [web:4] | Market dependence amplifies shocks [web:1] |

## Supporting Research Evidence
- **World Bank (2015)**: Informal/rural workers face poverty risks from low pay and no protections; 90% low-paid jobs informal.[web:2]
- **BARMM Economic Briefer (2024)**: Informal workers vulnerable to shocks without social protection or financial access.[web:1]
- **UP Diliman Studies**: Informality hinders decent work, poverty eradication; globalization boosts exports but unskilled job destruction.[web:3][web:4][web:7]
- **ILO Reports**: Decent work deficits pronounced in informal economy, needing legal recognition and protections.[web:6]
- **ASEAN/Policy Briefs**: Informal workers hit hardest in recessions; calls for expanded social security.[web:5][web:8][web:9]

## Policy Implications
Research urges formalization via simplified registration, targeted social protection, and Bangsamoro Labor Code enforcement.[web:1][web:9] Strategies include expanding benefits to informal workers and facilitating transitions to formal jobs.[web:5][web:9] Prioritizing these reduces deficits and builds resilience.[web:6]
