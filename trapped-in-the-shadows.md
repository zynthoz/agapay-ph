# Trapped in the Shadows – Informal Work's Grip on Filipino Lives (IBON Foundation Insights)

This report examines the reality of informal employment in the Philippines based on research by the IBON Foundation. The analysis highlights how recent official reports of a "rise in employment" may actually indicate deepening economic vulnerability, characterized by an increase in unstable jobs and a decline in overall employment quality. This phenomenon underscores a significant decent work deficit affecting millions of Filipinos.

## The False Dawn of Employment Statistics
The IBON Foundation's breakdown of official data reveals a stark economic picture. While employment numbers increased and underemployment dropped by February 2025, this shift is largely driven by individuals entering unstable, informal work out of necessity. Current estimates indicate 20.6 million openly informal workers. This includes 13.9 million self-employed individuals, 4.4 million workers in family farms or businesses (3.3 million of whom are unpaid), and 2.3 million domestic workers lacking formal protections.

These figures represent survival-driven labor rather than genuine economic gains. Research suggests that focusing purely on statistical employment growth overlooks the broader shift away from decent work towards informal labor. Workers in this sector face a lack of job security, highly volatile incomes, and an absence of formal benefits. This vulnerability is compounded by the lack of social safety nets, leaving workers exposed during health crises without systematic protections like PhilHealth or the Social Security System (SSS).

## Crisis Amplifier and Mobility Killer
Economic shocks such as the COVID-19 pandemic disproportionately impacted informal workers. IBON's analysis confirms that the absence of financial buffers leads to severe economic distress during systemic shocks. Furthermore, upward mobility remains severely restricted due to low skill levels and a lack of access to training programs. The effects of globalization have also played a role by driving contractualization, which reduces the availability of formal employment and leaves overseas work (OFWs) as one of the few avenues for advancement—a path that carries its own risks due to global economic fluctuations.

Observations from local vendor sectors align with these findings, highlighting stagnant growth in manufacturing and a shrinking labor force that obscures "disguised unemployment." Estimates pointing to over 70-80% of workers in underpaid informal roles indicate a persistent cycle of poverty.

## Policy Recommendations
Addressing this systemic issue requires moving beyond statistical employment tracking toward actionable economic policies. Key recommendations from the research include rebuilding local agriculture and industry to generate formal employment. Policy interventions should prioritize formalization strategies, such as expanding access to microfinance, vocational training, and social protection services, modeled after localized interventions that could potentially scale to a national level.
