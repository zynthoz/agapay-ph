# IBON's Wake-Up Call – When "Jobs" Mean Desperation in the Philippines

Research by the IBON Foundation provides a critical examination of the informal workforce crisis in the Philippines. As an independent economic think tank, IBON's analysis looks beyond headline employment statistics to uncover a severe lack of decent work. A substantial portion of the labor force—comprising street vendors, drivers, and home-based laborers—remains trapped in employment characterized by near-zero security, erratic compensation, and an absence of formal benefits.

## Survival, Not Success
IBON's data indicates that despite reports of "rising employment," the rate of informality is actually expanding due to a scarcity of formal jobs. The workforce is increasingly dominated by self-employed workers and unpaid family laborers who remain dangerously exposed to economic volatility without the backing of PhilHealth, SSS, or unemployment assistance. This lack of social protection means that a single unforeseen event, such as a natural disaster or major health issue, can completely deplete household savings. Additionally, upward mobility is heavily constrained by low skill levels and inadequate professional training. Within the context of globalization, labor contractualization has accelerated, pushing more citizens toward low-wage domestic traps or overseas labor markets.

Ground-level observations during systemic crises corroborate IBON's warnings regarding the acute vulnerability of the informal sector.

## Beyond the Numbers
To address the root causes of this crisis, IBON recommends genuine economic restructuring, specifically prioritizing agro-industrial revival and the implementation of robust, inclusive social protections. Measuring economic health must involve examining the quality and stability of employment, rather than simply tracking the absolute number of occupied individuals.
