# No Safety Nets, No Future – LGU Spotlights on Informal Struggles

Local Government Units (LGUs), particularly highlighted by insights from the BARMM region, are documenting the significant challenges faced by informal workers regarding decent work. The LGU Economic Briefer (Issue 4, 2024) provides a detailed analysis of how a lack of job security, volatile incomes, and missing employee benefits directly contribute to economic vulnerability among the population. Reports from local districts underscore the daily struggles of micro-vendors and freelancers confronting ongoing economic instability.

## Everyday Realities: The LGU Lens
LGU reports emphasize the dominance of informal labor in rural areas, characterized by low-paid temporary work that often falls outside minimum wage regulations. Earnings in these sectors are highly susceptible to seasonal slumps, and workers are entirely excluded from formal insurance or pension systems. The lack of social protection leaves families highly vulnerable to illnesses or natural disasters. As observed during recent crises, the absence of safety nets directly correlated with sharp spikes in poverty levels.

The limitation of social mobility further solidifies this economic trap; the absence of institutional training programs prevents workers from transitioning into higher-skilled, formal roles. Furthermore, globalization factors, such as labor flexibilization and heavy reliance on Overseas Filipino Worker (OFW) remittances, highlight the inadequacy of domestic job creation.

## LGU Paths Forward
In response, proactive LGUs are promoting strategic interventions to begin formalizing segments of the informal economy. Recommended initiatives include the expansion of microfinance programs, investment in vocational skills training, and the rollout of targeted social protections. Enforcing localized labor codes, similar to the Bangsamoro Labor law, provides a viable framework for establishing employment stability. Such localized strategies are crucial in addressing the root causes of the decent work deficit and building economic resilience.
