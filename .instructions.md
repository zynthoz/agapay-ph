# Agent Instructions: Poverty in the Contemporary World Project

## Design Context

### Users
**High school students (14-18)** in an in-class learning environment. They're encountering poverty through an academic lens—moving beyond simplistic "globalization" framings to understand complex interconnections, power dynamics, and agency. Students need clear scaffolding to grasp structural concepts while maintaining engagement and emotional connection to the human reality of poverty.

### Brand Personality
**Informative, engaging, and impactful.** The tone balances academic credibility with approachability—speak with authority about complex systems, but remain accessible to young learners. The personality avoids condescension while acknowledging the serious emotional weight of the topic.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Visual style**: Clean and minimal, letting content shine without decoration
- **Theme**: Dark mode only—creates focused concentration and reduces eye strain during extended study sessions
- **Interaction**: Heavy use of thoughtful animations and micro-interactions that create delightful moments and encourage exploration—but never frivolous (every animation serves engagement or comprehension)
- **Emotional tone**: Visual design that feels hopeful rather than despairing—color and motion choices should suggest agency and possibility, not despair

### Design Principles
1. **Content First, Elegantly Supported**: Clean typography, generous spacing, and minimal visual noise let the academic content take center stage. Visual design should clarify, not distract.
2. **Animate with Purpose**: Every animation and interactive moment should either delight the student, aid comprehension (reveal connections, highlight key relationships), or encourage deeper engagement. No motion for its own sake.
3. **Dark Mode Thoughtfully**: Use dark backgrounds to create cognitive focus and reduce eye strain, with careful color contrast choices that maintain readability and emotional warmth.
4. **Accessible Complexity**: Make systems, actors, and global interdependence *visible* through thoughtful layout, visual hierarchy, and interactive explorations—help young minds grasp multi-layered concepts without overwhelming them.
5. **Emotional Honesty with Hope**: Design acknowledges the gravity of poverty without despair. Use color, motion, and visual metaphors that suggest change is possible and students' understanding matters.

---

## Project Notes

This is a webpage project designed to help high school students understand poverty through a contemporary global perspective, moving beyond traditional globalization frameworks. Content is structured for in-class use and explores poverty through the lens of key global actors, structural causes, and multiple pathways forward.
